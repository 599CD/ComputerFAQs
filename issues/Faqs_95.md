Q: In Microsoft Word, how can I create that neat effect that you have in your newsletter, where the text is white in a black box? > Joseph, Buffalo NY

A: The easiest way to do it is to select your text, use the Highlighter tool on the toolbar to make the background black, and then change the foreground color to white. However, you can also click on Format > Borders & Shading and create a nifty border effect and background shading.

Q: In MS Access, can you freeze the columns of a table, like you can in MS Excel? > Mike, Buffalo NY

A: Yes, you can. Simply highlight the column you wish to "freeze" and click on Format > Freeze Columns. Now when you scroll left and right, everything to the left of that column stays put.

Q: When I receive an email, I am sometimes asked if I want to send a "return receipt" to the sender. How can I turn off this annoying feature? > Nick, Depew NY

A: In Outlook 2000, click on Tools > Options then on the Preferences tab click on Email Options. Click on the Tracking Options button. At the bottom, select "Never send a response." Click OK. You're all set.

Q: When I prepare a PowerPoint presentation, how can I set it so that it runs automatically and loops back to the beginning when it's done? > Cam, Toronto ON

A: After you set up your presentation, go into the Slide Sorter view and click on Slide Show > Rehearse Timings. Run through your slide show, pausing as long as you think necessary on each slide, and clicking to advance through the slides. When finished, you'll be prompted to record the slide timings. Say "yes." Now, click on Slide Show > Set Up Show. Click on the box that says "Loop continuously until Esc." When you run your slide show now, it will loop until you hit the Escape key. We teach this in our Microsoft PowerPoint Complete class.

Q: I would like to copy a value from one place on an Excel spreadsheet and paste just the value in another location, but whenever I try, I get the formula in that cell, which is wrong. Help. > Alice, Buffalo NY

A: What you need to do is select the cell, copy it to the clipboard, and then right-click on the cell you wish to paste and select Paste Special. From the menu that appears, click on Values from the top "Paste" section. This will place just the value from the previous cell into the new cell. We teach this in Excel Intermediate.
